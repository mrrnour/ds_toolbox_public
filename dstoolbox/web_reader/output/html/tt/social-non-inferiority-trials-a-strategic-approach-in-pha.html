I was thinking the other day—why do pharma companies spend millions on a clinical trial just to prove a new drug isn't better than what's already out there? It sounds completely backwards, but then I get to know non-inferiority trials are a brilliant strategy. They aren't meant to find a superior drug. Instead, they just prove that if the new treatment is slightly less effective, the drop is minor enough to stay within a clinically acceptable margin. This is a huge win when the new drug brings other massive perks to the table, like swapping an injection for a once-daily pill, cutting down side effects, or making treatment way more affordable for patients. I recently re-read Seokyung Hahn’s paper on this (PMC3510268) and decided to map out a quick visual guide to break down the core logic, the flipped hypotheses, and how to interpret those tricky confidence intervals.
